## Gap

`gap` sets the separation between items as a step on the space scale, from `0` for none to `12`. The default is `4`. Each row below holds the same three avatars at one step. An inline lays out one row; use `Stack` for a column.

<Example name="gap" wide />

## Alignment

`align` positions items across the row, along the block axis. The default is `center`. `start` and `end` put each item on the top or bottom edge of the row, and `stretch` makes every item as tall as the tallest one, which is visible on the badge and the button.

<Example name="align" />

## Baseline

`baseline` aligns items by the baseline of their text, where `center` aligns their boxes. Use it when items carry text of different sizes, such as a label, a figure and a note.

<Example name="baseline" />

## Justification

`justify` positions items along the row and distributes the free space between them: `start`, `center`, `end`, `between`, `around` or `evenly`. Without `justify`, items sit at the start. `between` pushes the first and last items to the edges.

<Example name="justify" />

## Wrapping

`wrap` defaults to `wrap`: items that do not fit move to the next line. `nowrap` keeps one line: the items shrink as far as they can and overflow a container narrower than that. The badges here shorten their text, so `nowrap` suits items that stay readable when they shrink.

<Example name="wrap" />

## Another element

An inline paints nothing and claims no semantics, so you choose the element. In React, `render` replaces the root element. In Angular, the `slInline` attribute goes on the element itself. A `ul` keeps the bullets and the indentation the browser gives it: an inline does not reset them. A row of fields with a submit button is a `form`, and `align="end"` sets the button on the bottom edge of the fields.
