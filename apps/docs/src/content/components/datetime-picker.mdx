## Typing or choosing

The field and the panel write the same value. Type the month, day, year, hour and minute into the segments, or press the calendar button, choose a day in the calendar and a time in the clock, and press Apply. The time can also be typed at the head of the clock. Each change reports its reason: `keyboard` for typing, `select` for a moment applied from the panel, `clear` for the clear button and `blur` for a value completed when the field loses focus. Press `+`, `-` or `=` on a segment to type a relative expression instead, such as `+2h` or `+1d`, measured from `referenceDateTime`.

<Example name="typing" />

## Completing the value

The panel holds a draft: a day and a time are two presses, and Apply writes both and closes the panel. Apply stays disabled while a half is missing. A day pressed with no time moves focus to the clock. Escape and a press outside leave the value as it was. `referenceDateTime` states the moment it is: the month an empty panel opens on, the distance written over the clock, and what Now and the steps from now count from. Unset, it is the current time.

<Example name="reference" />

## 12-hour and 24-hour clocks

`hourCycle` takes `12` or `24` and applies to the field, the clock and the date and time in words. Unset, the locale decides: `en-US` writes 12-hour times with AM and PM, `en-GB` and `de-DE` write 24-hour times.

<Example name="hour-cycle" />

## Step between times

`step` sets the seconds between two times in the clock, 900 by default. The clock counts from the earliest time the day allows: the time of `min` on its own day, `minTime` on the other days, and midnight when neither is set. A step of 1800 from a `minTime` of 7:15 AM offers 7:15, 7:45 and 8:15. The step applies to the clock only: the field accepts any minute by typing and by the arrow keys.

<Example name="step" />

## Hours, minutes or seconds

`granularity` sets how much of the time the field and the clock write: `hour`, `minute` (the default) or `second`. With `second`, the clock adds a grid of seconds under the minutes.

<Example name="granularity" />

## Stated times

`options` replaces the step with your own list of times, for a schedule that no step describes, such as departures or delivery windows. The clock draws whole times under the part of the day each one is in, in the order you give. The bounds and the predicates still refuse times in it, and there is no Now and no step from now.

<Example name="options" />

## Earliest and latest

Two pairs of bounds apply together. `min` and `max` are whole date-times: where the range starts and where it ends. `minTime` and `maxTime` bound the times of every day inside the range. On the first day the clock starts at the time of `min`, on the last day it stops at the time of `max`, and the calendar disables the days outside the range. A value outside the bounds stays in the field and marks the datetime picker invalid.

<Example name="bounds" />

## Unavailable dates and times

`isDateUnavailable` receives each date and returns `true` for a day the reader cannot choose; the calendar disables it and its clock offers no time. `isTimeUnavailable` receives a time and the date it belongs to, so the rule can differ by day. The clock follows the day in the draft, and refused hours and minutes stay in place, disabled.

<Example name="unavailable" />

## Time zones and wall-clock values

The value is a `PlainDateTime`: a `date` and a `time` with no time zone, the time a clock on the wall shows. `toISODateTime` writes it as `2026-09-30T14:00:00`, the format of `input[type=datetime-local]`, and `parseISODateTime` reads it back. When your server stores instants, `wallClockIn` from `@slotted/core/date` converts an instant to the wall-clock time in a time zone; converting back to an instant stays in your application. `timeZone` decides which day the calendar marks as today and what the Now shortcut writes. Stated, the zone is written over the clock. Unset, both come from the browser's time zone.

<Example name="time-zones" />

## Locale and format

`locale` takes a BCP 47 tag and sets the order of the segments, their separators and placeholders, the clock, the month and weekday names in the calendar, and the date and time in words that the calendar button announces. There is no format string: each locale writes its own shape. Unset, the locale comes from `SlottedProvider` in React or `provideSlotted` in Angular, and falls back to `en-US`. The value is the same `PlainDateTime` in every locale.

<Example name="locale" />

## Sizes

`size` sets the field and the trigger to one step of the control scale: `sm` for toolbars and filter rows, `md` for most forms, `lg` for sparse forms. Inside a field, an unset size follows the field.

<Example name="sizes" />

## Variants

`outline` is the default frame. `subtle` fills the control, for a form on a tinted surface. `plain` draws no frame at rest, for a table cell or a toolbar, and still shows hover and focus.

<Example name="variants" />

## States

Set `invalid`, `readOnly` and `disabled` on the field, which passes them to the datetime picker; each can also be set on the datetime picker itself. Invalid draws the frame and the focus ring in the danger tone and sets `aria-invalid`. Read-only keeps the value legible and focusable. Read-only and disabled both disable the calendar button, so the panel cannot open.

<Example name="states" />

## Controlled

Pass `value` and update it from `onValueChange` in React, or bind `[value]` and update it from `(valueChange)` in Angular, when other parts of the screen depend on the value. The handler receives a `PlainDateTime`, or `null` while the field is empty or holds only one half. Buttons outside the datetime picker can set the value; the field and the panel follow.

<Example name="controlled" />

## Clearing

`clearable` adds a clear button to the field while it holds any part of a value. The button empties both halves, reports `null` with the reason `clear` and returns focus to the field.

<Example name="clearable" />

## Confirmation and footer

The panel keeps a draft until Apply, and Clear empties it. `confirm={false}` in React, or `confirm="false"` in Angular, drops Apply: each press on a whole moment writes it, and the press on the finest unit closes the panel. `footer` in React and an element with `slDateTimePickerFooter` in Angular set the text beside the actions; unset, it names the step.

<Example name="footer" />

## In a form

`name` adds a hidden input that submits the value as an ISO string, such as `2026-10-01T08:00:00`, whether it was typed or chosen. In Angular, the datetime picker is a form control: `formControlName`, `formControl` and `ngModel` bind the value, `Validators.required` applies to it, and the datetime picker reports `slottedDateTimeRange` outside `min` and `max`, `slottedDateTimeWindow` outside `minTime` and `maxTime`, `slottedDateTimeUnavailable` for a refused date or time and `slottedDateTimeIncomplete` while only one half is typed. In React, the value stays in your state and you validate it on submit. Submit without a value to see the required error, type a date in September to see the bound error, then reset.

<Example name="form" />

## Keyboard and the trigger's name

Alt+ArrowDown opens the panel from the field and leaves focus in the field, so typing keeps working. The calendar button opens it with Enter, Space or ArrowDown and moves focus to the chosen day. Tab and Shift+Tab move between the shortcuts, the calendar, the clock and the footer until the panel closes. In the clock, the arrow keys move between hours and between minutes, and Home and End move to the first and last one. Enter or Space chooses, and Ctrl+Enter applies. Escape closes the panel, discards the draft and returns focus to the calendar button; while the calendar shows months or years, Escape first returns to the days. The button's accessible name carries the chosen date and time, and `messages` replaces it: `choose` while no value is set and `change`, with the value in its `{date}` slot, once one is. `onOpenChange` in React and `(openChange)` in Angular report why the panel opened or closed.

<Example name="keyboard" />

## Narrow screens

Under 760 pixels, the panel shows the month or the clock, one at a time. The day and the time stay written at the head of each, and a press on a head brings its part; a press on a day moves to the clock. The shortcuts become a strip. Under 480 pixels on a touch screen, the panel opens as a sheet attached to the bottom of the viewport, and the positioner carries `data-presentation="sheet"`.

<Example name="narrow" />

## Carrier pickup slot

A carrier collects in four fixed windows on weekdays, and each window takes three trucks. `options` lists the windows, `isTimeUnavailable` refuses a full window on its own day, and `renderSlot` in React or an `ng-template` with `slClockSlot` in Angular writes the trucks left in each one. The slot receives the time, its label, its state and the day in the draft. The button keeps its role, its states and its keyboard; only its content is yours.

## Publish at a set time

A price list goes live at a set time in New York. `min` stops the reader before the moment the form opened, `presets` adds the two times the team uses most after Now, and `timeZone` makes today and Now follow New York. The value stays a wall-clock time; the description names the zone, so the reader and your server read it the same way.
